Section 02
Communications Protocols

Communication at JDJ Systems keeps us aligned, efficient and connected. We operate two modes of communication and have clear guidelines on when to use each.

Synchronous Communication

The primary driver of collaboration and team cohesion. We leverage real-time interaction through office workshops and virtual sessions to ensure every colleague — regardless of location — has access to collective team alignment. Virtual coffee chats are encouraged to build professional connections.

Asynchronous Communication

Mastering async tools is essential to avoid dysfunction while ensuring efficiency, autonomy and hybrid flexibility. Every discussion, decision, design and project work is documented in a knowledge base — minimising burnout and encouraging clear objectives.

Section 03
Decision Structure

All decisions begin with a proposal — when a team member identifies an opportunity for improvement or project enhancement. Every proposal must include: a clear description, a business impact assessment, and a review process with relevant stakeholders.

Direct Commit

Low-Impact Changes

When: Automation after approval; naming, formatting and routine requirements met; rollback allowed without affecting production.

Process: Deciding member reviews, tags #MARKED APPROVE and merges. Automated document of changes lodged to SharePoint.

Log: Name of task and proposed changes

Pull Request

Code & Workflow Changes

When: Changes to code, workflow scripts or tooling; improving work processes.

Process: Team member proposes and documents to SharePoint. Async discussion — if approved, moves forward; if not, an RFC is developed.

Log: Proposal, reviewers, decision stakeholders; SharePoint, Jira

RFC

Breaking Changes

When: Alters direction, workflow or architecture. Must be completed by the whole team.

Process: Structured async contribution. If no decision is reached, sync meetings are held. Project Lead holds final say after PoC testing.

Log: Direction, rationale, different views on record; SharePoint, development platform

Raising Objections

Every request or proposal has an owner, a start and completion date. When a blocker is raised, it must contain valid reasons for clarity and contextualisation. After a consensus decision, concerns must be documented in SharePoint and acknowledged by the project lead — even when results do not change.

Acknowledging All Voices

The project lead actively tags all relevant members — discussions are mandatory for all to contribute. Any objections should be tabled and discussed. Anonymous contributions carry equal weight and consideration.

Section 04
Conflict Resolution

When conflicts arise over scope, architecture or direction — through professional disagreement, repeated misinterpretation or withdrawal — the project lead is responsible for identifying these and taking the necessary action to resolve them.

Team Differences

Team members bring different cultural defaults. Every member must separate spoken or written words from the person — uniformity must not be assumed when dealing with interpersonal issues.

Assume Positive Intent

When conflict erupts, start with curiosity and seek clarity. Avoid raising public concerns on Slack. Private exchanges must be respectful, professional and addressed constructively.

Escalation

If a private exchange has not resolved the issue, notify the project lead. Either concerned party may make the request — it cannot be declined. The project lead will facilitate with HR present.

Section 05
Accountability & Expectation

Task Assignment & Tracking

All work items are assigned a task owner with full responsibility. Tasks are managed in a Kanban Jira board (To Do → In Progress → Done). In-Progress tasks have a 48-hour status update requirement, auto-tracked to Slack.

Contribution & Participation

Every team member is a leader in their deliverables. Members must self-manage workload, proactively respond to blockers, and maintain shared accountability of outcomes.

Workload Equity

Workload is formally reviewed during weekly sync sprint meetings. Imbalances are addressed by reallocation. Any member can raise concerns without vilification.

Addressing Performance Issues

The project lead intervenes first through a private check-in. Further issues are escalated to HR. Public callouts are strongly prohibited.

Responsiveness Standards

Responses to team communication are expected within 24 hours on business days. Availability notices should be posted in #Notification with 24hr to 1 week prior notice.
